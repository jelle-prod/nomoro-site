Buffer asks you four smart questions during onboarding.

Then acts like you never answered them.

I signed up this week and recorded it. Credit first: the segmentation flow is better than most. One question per screen, skippable at every step, a progress dial, no wall of form fields. Somebody thought about it.

I told it I manage 21 to 50 accounts and that I already use a competitor.

Two of the strongest signals a new user can give you.

What came next was a generic dashboard and a static pricing grid. Nothing acknowledged what I had just said.

And before I could connect anything, a modal appeared over the checklist asking how I heard about Buffer.

Same problem twice. Both moments collect information for someone inside the company. Neither helps the person trying to connect their first channel.

Here is what acting on the answer can look like.

At Hootsuite we had the same problem in Analytics. A new user landed on an empty product and had to connect accounts and wait for data before anything was worth looking at. So we seeded the account with demo data. You could open a template, move things around, find an actual insight, and understand what the product was for before you had connected anything of your own.

The aha moment stopped depending on setup.

That is the difference between asking a question and using the answer. Buffer already knows I run 21 to 50 accounts and that I am coming from somewhere else. That is enough to show me a workspace that looks like mine before I have built it.

An onboarding survey is not free, even when it is well designed. You are spending goodwill to collect it. If nothing downstream changes, you have taught someone their answers do not matter, at the exact moment you wanted them to trust you.

What does a new user see in your product before they have connected anything?
